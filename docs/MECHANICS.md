# Mechanical Slice

## Proximity Coverage

The eight `proximity.distances` readings are **individual rays**, spaced by pi/4 around the robot base, not sectors or a 360-degree free-space certificate. Each now includes `origin_base_m`: the ray starts where its bearing intersects a fixed nominal base rectangle (half extents 0.18 m forward/back and 0.25 m lateral), at base-relative z = -0.115 m. With the nominal 0.155 m level base height this is approximately 0.04 m above the floor; the actual origin follows measured base position/orientation. The sensor origins do not expand with the arms.

`distance_m` is measured from that ray's origin, not the robot centre, arm tip or closest whole-body surface. A `hit` reports a detected intersection within 2 m; `clear` with null distance means no hit in that ray's finite coverage, not infinite clearance. `occluded` means the robot itself intercepted the ray, and also carries null rather than invented free distance. Obstacles between rays or above/below their height can be missed.

A front-left reading of 0.31 m therefore cannot by itself determine whether a turn is safe. The current whole-robot footprint includes the arms, observed maps inflate occupied and unknown cells, and buffered motion separately checks the swept whole-body geometry (including rotation) through the existing collision shield. This privileged simulator shield is not part of Luna's map/image evidence and is not a hardware safety certification. Actual sensor/ray definitions are in [simulation.py](../backend/simulation.py); freshness and clock contracts are documented in [HOME_MAPPING.md](HOME_MAPPING.md#authorization-and-observation-provenance).

## Travelled Path

**World view > Travelled path** projects recent sampled robot-base positions as a blue line on the ground. It shows where the robot has travelled since this view opened, not its intended route, navigable space or a full-body swept footprint. It uses the existing operator world-position stream only; no trail data or spectator geometry is sent to Luna or used for motion authorization.

The switch defaults on and remembers its choice in the same tab. Hiding the path preserves collection; changing missions or spectator quality preserves the trail. Scene/episode reset and page reload clear it. Collection is bounded to 4,096 segments with at least 0.02 m displacement per segment. Manual placement, missing connection/pose, power-off, significant time gaps and position discontinuities start a new disconnected segment. Unobserved gaps are never connected into an invented path. The flat-floor projection uses a small height offset and depth testing, so obstacles can occlude it. Complete recorded trajectories remain available separately in the test archive. [Implementation and validation](DESIGN_PERFORMANCE.md).

## Movement Zones

The opt-in **World view > Sensors & areas** overlay uses `policy-clearance-v1` corridor sampling and `moving-overlay-v1` display anchoring. It requires the current backend after restart. It is operator-only: no new information is added to AgentObservation, head-camera images, model prompts or motion commands. Enabling it adds `X-Milo-Motion-Zones: 1` to the existing spatial-data poll, rather than starting another polling loop. Normal control responses do not compute or include these zones. The switch is retained across reloads in the same browser tab.

- **Black outline:** conservative robot-base collision footprint from the last matching area snapshot, including its arm posture and shape margins, displayed at the live base pose. This is not just the chassis rectangle or an independently refreshed posture sensor.
- **Blue circle:** the map-planning radius including its existing grid clearance margin: radius + 0.10 m for the home map, radius + 0.025 m for the rolling depth map. The rolling controller can retain the radius from its route's starting posture. Conservative circular inflation can reject narrow corridors that a more capable orientation-aware planner might negotiate; this is not evidence of a distance-unit error.
- **0.25 / 0.50 / 1.00 m bands:** sampled possible robot-center travel, split into sixteen headings. Every band summarizes samples from the center to its outer radius, not only the annulus. Coordinates use 0 degrees forward and positive angles toward the robot's left. Samples are half a map cell apart. These are map-admission previews, not an exact swept-volume or route-search result.
- **Green:** all sampled positions in that corridor pass the current observed footprint-clearance mask. **Red:** a measured obstacle or inflated obstacle/unknown margin restricts the corridor. **Gray:** unobserved/out-of-map samples prevent a conclusion. **Amber:** sensing, localization, receipt freshness or pose alignment no longer supports a current preview. Hover a region for its reason, or open **Zone readings / center travel** for a touch/keyboard-accessible table.
- **Live rays:** eight measured low-mounted sensor beams, red for a hit and blue for no return within the finite 2 m range. A red ray does not necessarily imply a braking threshold violation. Occluded rays are not drawn as clear; stale/disconnected/off rays are gray. Rays follow the live base independently of sampled map-area freshness. **Red ticks:** the sampled distance-stop threshold on directions checked by the buffered command, held at the area snapshot pose. They are measured from each ray's sensor mount, not from the robot center. During idle, a labelled 0.15 m/s forward/reverse reference is shown; it is not a commanded speed. At runtime the preview uses the greater magnitude of commanded/current linear velocity, so it is conservative when those differ.

The existing beam stop formula is `max(0.18, v*v/(2*0.3) + abs(v)*0.05 + 0.08)` metres, using the 0.3 m/s2 acceleration limit. It requires 0.18 m at 0.15 m/s, about 0.302 m at 0.35 m/s and about 0.522 m at 0.50 m/s. The continuous tracker separately caps forward speed at 0.20 m/s when a front beam sees an obstacle within 1 m, and computes another braking-speed bound; that is slowing, not a global motion prohibition. For heading error at least 0.7 rad it rotates before translating. These existing rules were reviewed, not relaxed.

Green never overrides an operator Stop, a missing mission objective, an expired buffer, stale depth/localization, floor support, tilt, held-object restrictions or the privileged whole-body collision shield. The overlay does not reproduce the shield's dynamic-obstacle prediction or all rotation/arm clearances. The legend retains the controller's last stop code separately, so BUFFER_EXPIRED is not presented as an obstacle. Areas, planning radius and stop ticks stay at the world pose supplied by the operator-only snapshot instead of moving old clearance with the robot. Motion alone does not invalidate an anchored area snapshot; old servers without display pose retain the 0.05 m / 0.08 rad guard. Mismatched episodes, missing/error/paused telemetry, off/disconnected state and source freshness expiry still suppress green/red area colors. Home-map validity also uses the controller's 0.75 s laser-sample and 2 s consistency-check limits. Browser elapsed time plus the full spatial-request duration are conservatively added to reported age; absolute clocks are not mixed. Live beam status separately requires proximity simulation time within 0.1 s of the physical snapshot and a received proximity update within 750 ms. These are display conditions, never safety gates.

When the overlay is shown and the robot is working, the single-flight spatial poll waits at least 250 ms after each response, or four times that request's duration if larger. This backs off expensive/delayed requests. Hidden-overlay working mode remains 750 ms, idle 2 s and power-off 5 s. It does not increase actual sensor capture resolution, range or frequency. The legend reports area and beam states separately because their update and expiry times differ.

The distance/footprint tests found no calibration defect in their fixtures. The recorded real-Luna renewal stall remains unresolved: a different fresh-map, sensor-only probe moved successfully, so it does not establish that the saved-map/model workload is fixed. [Review, probe and qualification evidence](DESIGN_PERFORMANCE.md).

## Motion-Camera History

`CameraHistory` consumes already accepted paired head-camera RGB frames in the owning worker; moving-proposal captures also feed it. It adds no renderer or model invocation to the existing target-5-Hz sensor loop. History stores the original PNG with only capture sequence/time, measured head angles, odometry and image size. No spectator state, object IDs, private geometry, depth-based motion ticket or evaluator state is stored in its model-facing metadata.

The short ring retains 50 frames (about ten seconds at nominal cadence). A separate pool preserves up to six representative views across the interval since the last acknowledged Luna review, with a 300-second age ceiling. Selection uses measured translation, wrapped viewing heading, head pitch and local image-patch differences. A small fingerprint is used only for selection; the original images are not resized. When over capacity, temporal endpoints and diverse views are preferred. This is a bounded heuristic: tiny/subtle objects, occlusion, similar appearances and dropped/unsampled frames can still be missed.

A review offers at most four views in chronological left-to-right/top-to-bottom order, plus metadata for up to six retained choices. Native 160x120 views occupy a 332x332 PNG including captions/gaps. There is no cropping or downscaling of those source pixels. The current image is separate at its existing resolution with candidate annotations; the previous duplicate raw/current image and separately attached panorama images are no longer sent in continuous mode. Ordinary requests therefore have one current image and zero or one sheet. No sheet is sent without distinct history. Old views carry frame IDs, simulated capture times, headings, age and explicit `historical=true` / `authorizes_motion=false` metadata.

Reviews are acknowledged only after a valid live response. Up to six distinct frames captured while that response is pending are retained separately, then merged into the next interval; they are not erased by the acknowledgement. Unchanged inference-time frames are suppressed. Up to 24 offered originals remain retrievable until age/capacity eviction. Each stored PNG is bounded to 256,000 bytes and calibrated dimensions no larger than 640x480. Buffers are in memory, bounded and episode-local; disabling/resetting sensing, manual relocation and worker shutdown invalidate them and their generation-specific IDs.

`guide_continuous(action="inspect_history", history_frame_id="...")` requests one offered original through the validated worker. It does not move the robot. The next request can contain the current image, optional sheet and that unchanged original (at most three images). Expired/unknown IDs return an unavailable result. An original-image response may request look/turn/scan/wait; destination, circling and completion selections are deferred until a subsequent fresh-view decision. Historical-original responses cannot create a current-position place sighting or trigger automatic exploration recovery. All eventual motion still uses current depth/candidates and the original freshness, clearance, lease, budget and Stop guards.

Stop revisions protect review, acknowledgement and original retrieval. Reset, takeover, disconnect and instruction changes retain the existing cancellation semantics; late replies cannot execute. History does not increase the model-call cadence, start a second inference loop, or interrupt long local actions such as a circuit. Those actions still finish or reach their existing boundaries before Luna reviews additional views. An inspection request consumes ordinary response/time budget. Actual recognition benefit, model latency and image-token savings require a separate real-model comparison; a contact sheet is not automatically cheaper than separate images for every model. Legacy single-step, voice and SmolVLA camera batching remain unchanged.

## Furniture Circuits

`furniture_circuit` is a separate 14 x 12 m room containing a four-legged wooden table, blue sofa, yellow chair and red-shaded floor lamp. `POST /api/challenges/load` accepts `orbit_target` and `orbit_direction` only for this preset. Loading defaults to table/clockwise; reset preserves the loaded options. Furniture colliders and visual geometry are shared by the physics and both views. Private target centers and scoring radii are excluded from `Challenge.public()` and model observations.

Luna's continuous `circle` decision identifies one current-image bounding box, object label and direction. `ObservedOrbit` projects valid paired depth within that box into wheel-odometry coordinates, filters floor/background surfaces, and estimates a center and clearance radius. This is an approximate visible-surface estimate, not segmentation, persistent object tracking or proof of identity. Ambiguous/occluded objects require inspection or approach. No fixture-position lookup supplies the controller's target.

The validated worker requires stowed arms, empty grippers, same episode/Stop revision, a recent stationary selection and fresh map coverage. It executes an approach followed by short curved paths, each within the existing 1.8 m/60-second local limits. The entire circle routine is bounded by 180 wall seconds and 32 segment attempts. Unknown/blocked arcs permit at most two rescans: the base turns toward the measured next arc, then the existing floor scan covers that travel direction. The target and original deadline are retained. Unknown space is never declared clear, and Stop/takeover/reset/disconnect invalidate pending motion. Existing acceleration, angular speed, sensor-age limits and privileged whole-body safety shield are unchanged. The 0.5 m/s open-space command cap slows near obstacles and at route endpoints; this is not uninterrupted or minimum-time circling.

An independent scorer uses actual world XY, floor support, speed and contact measurements, sampled every 12 physics ticks. Progress requires net winding in the requested direction inside the selected object's private radius band. Travel and near-start closure reject spinning, wrong-object loops and partial arcs; implausible jumps or displacement without simulated-time advancement reset progress. Contact latches failure. A full lap must end below 0.025 m/s and 0.15 rad/s for 0.5 simulated seconds. There is no minimum-speed success threshold. Contact sampling is not a per-tick collision guarantee.

For this scenario, the continuous supervisor terminates on the independent completed/failed state; model `finish` cannot substitute for it. Evaluator coordinates and progress do not enter navigation inputs. Editing the free-text robot goal or an active instruction does not change the loaded scoring target: load the desired object/direction first to change the scored task. Other controller modes and other scenarios retain their existing completion behavior.

## Graphics Snapshot Pilot

Newly launched FastAPI apps default to Enhanced for the spectator, head RGB and paired spatial RGB/depth. Enhanced uses shared Three.js physical materials, local bitmap bump maps, a generated room-lighting environment, ACES tone mapping, directional shadows, and rounded edges on selected fixtures. Rounded visuals remain within the original conservative box colliders. There are no new obstacles, high-detail replacement meshes, altered physics materials or changed task rules. The glass finish uses environment reflections, not a geometrically accurate mirror of the scene. Backend `rendering=enhanced` overrides old browser preferences and hides the spectator-only quality switch. Explicit `MILO_RENDERER=tiny` retains the previous live baseline; standalone simulator constructors keep their CPU default unless configured otherwise.

`backend.camera.scene_snapshot` exports `milo-render-v1` from the owning physics worker: run ID, epoch, observation sequence, complete visual geometry/link poses, simulation time and calibrated camera eye/target/up vectors. This is privileged renderer input, never an AgentObservation or training feature. Export neither steps physics nor advances observation sequence. The live worker creates immutable snapshots serially; the offline benchmark owns a separate isolated simulator.

`frontend/src/gpuCamera.ts` applies poses exactly, without spectator interpolation, to render RGB and optical-axis metric depth from the same frozen snapshot. It retains the 65-degree vertical field of view, 0.015 m near plane and 12 m far plane. The depth pass uses an unlit packed linear-distance shader, read back as float32 little-endian meters with NaN for no return or self-mask pixels, including a one-pixel self-mask margin. The live adapter filters metric depth to the existing sensor's near/usable-range limits and emits the unchanged SpatialObservation schema. Bump shading affects RGB normals, not depth geometry. Run/epoch mismatches reject capture; offline replay within the loaded episode is permitted. Renderer identity checks supplement, not replace, the worker's capture-age and motion-authorization checks.

The `?cameraWorker=1` entry point creates only a renderer: no live WebSocket, controller, model inference or physics stepping. The benchmark launches its own headless Edge process and ephemeral loopback asset server, then closes both. No operator browser tab is needed. Only local texture paths are accepted; texture loading has a ten-second bound. Shared assets, materials and lighting are used by both the enhanced spectator and headless camera. Lighting seed zero selects nominal settings; a positive seed deterministically varies exposure and key-light position while leaving geometry/depth unchanged.

Build, then choose a fresh output directory:

```powershell
npm --prefix frontend run build
./.runtime/env/python.exe -m scripts.benchmark_rendering --output .runtime/my-rendering-check --samples 10
./.runtime/env/python.exe -m scripts.benchmark_rendering --output .runtime/my-varied-rendering-check --samples 10 --lighting-seed 713
```

The comparison covers a calibration wall/occluder and three kitchen head poses at 320x240 and 640x480. It saves source snapshots, CPU/GPU RGB images, axial depth arrays, timings, a labelled comparison sheet and report. `--export-only` omits GPU execution. `--with-local-policy` optionally starts one isolated SmolVLA process for non-actuating load measurements, but skips it when less than 4,000 MiB of VRAM is free. No existing model is unloaded. The optional contention branch was skipped on the current workstation and has not been runtime-qualified. Repeated frozen-image predictions would measure load, not task accuracy.

The live adapter uses `scripts/render_camera.mjs --serve` over serialized local JSON pipes. It requires a hardware renderer, allows one request at a time, checks request/run/epoch/sequence/time/dimensions on replies and closes on timeout or malformed output. RGB-only calls skip the depth pass. The renderer and a separate one-worker spatial-processing process warm up before readiness, survive app episode resets, and close on app shutdown. Reset drains old preview/map jobs, constructs a new physics worker, clears caches and loads the new episode into the renderer. Separate app servers have separate resources.

Direct-action and policy previews use one asynchronous immutable snapshot at a time. Continuous sensing reuses the exact paired RGB as its live preview, avoiding a competing render. Preview age limits remain 0.5 seconds (operator) and one second (policy); older simulation frames cannot overwrite newer ones. Pre-Stop enhanced previews are discarded by Stop revision. Spatial mapping remains bounded to one outstanding result; copies are processed outside the physics process to avoid Python scheduling contention, and results older than the original one-second deadline remain rejected. No safety age limit, command lease, speed limit or stop authority was relaxed. A renderer fault stops the simulator and Resume refuses a closed/dead renderer until reset.

Recorders and model feedback consume the enhanced head images produced by their current worker. Privileged render packets never enter policy requests. Existing offline training files and checkpoints are unchanged; standalone scripts need `MILO_RENDERER=enhanced` explicitly to collect/evaluate the new appearance. Actual local-policy GPU contention and learned-task transfer are still unqualified. The initial offline benchmark below remains historical evidence, not a measurement of current live scheduling.

### Isaac Sim And Isaac Lab

Isaac Sim is a possible separate RTX rendering/PhysX simulation backend; Isaac Lab adds learning environments, vectorized rollouts, randomization, reinforcement-learning and imitation-learning workflows on top of Sim. Lab is not an alternative renderer and is unnecessary for a graphics-only pilot. Milo's [URDF](../assets/milo.urdf) can be imported as USD, but successful import alone would not validate wheel slip, drives, gripper contacts or task outcomes.

Recommended sequence: select a supported, compatible Sim/Lab version pair; run NVIDIA's compatibility checker; import one robot and the kitchen in an isolated process; check calibrated RGB/depth, contacts, motion limits, Stop/reset and deterministic task scoring; only then implement an optional backend adapter. Never run two physics engines as simultaneous authorities for the same robot. A snapshot-only RTX renderer is an alternative first stage with its own synchronization cost. Preserve the current PyBullet baseline and camera/AgentObservation allowlist.

For learned control, add a custom Isaac Lab environment only after the embodiment is calibrated. Actions, observations, rewards, resets, randomization, demonstrations and data adapters must be defined for Milo; existing SmolVLA weights and LeRobot data are not automatically compatible. ROS 2 integration can use Isaac Sim's bridge, with isolated topics/domain and explicit clock/reset semantics so it cannot command the current app unintentionally.

As checked on 2026-09-13, NVIDIA lists an RTX 4080 with 16 GB VRAM as the current Sim minimum; Lab's installation guidance lists 16 GB or more, with additional capacity for rendering/training. This workstation has an RTX 3080 with 10 GB, below those specifications. A small below-spec experiment is not ruled out, but neither compatibility nor a practical training workload is established. Native Windows 11 is supported; the Isaac Sim container is Linux-only. Use approved dependency and asset distribution routes on this managed device; no packages, containers, cloud resources or Isaac integrations were created by this pilot.

Sources: [Sim requirements](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/latest/installation/requirements.html), [URDF import](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/latest/importer_exporter/import_urdf.html), [ROS 2 bridge](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/latest/ros2_tutorials/index.html), [Lab installation](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/main/source/setup/installation/index.html), [Lab task workflows](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/main/source/overview/core-concepts/task_workflows.html).

## Pedestrian Crossing

The `pedestrian_crossing` preset contains a kinematic articulated-looking person, crosswalk markings and a green parking bay. The person starts after 0.30 m forward encoder travel with positive physical forward velocity, crosses 2.6 m at 0.65 m/s, then remains on the far side. Component poses update on each 240 Hz physics tick. Head RGB/depth, range rays, contact sensing and the spectator all observe those actual bodies. Reset constructs fresh crossing state; constructor settling never starts the actor.

Buffered navigation checks contacts, proximity, floor support and whole-robot clearance before each 12-tick motion block. Its additional moving-obstacle check uses current physical translation velocities, or finite-difference velocities for kinematic bodies, to predict 0.8 seconds in 0.1-second samples. The shadow robot follows the requested linear/angular velocity; a margin of 0.08 m plus 0.05 seconds of obstacle travel covers short sampling gaps. Existing static clearance checks remain. Moving-obstacle overlap raises `CLEARANCE_STOP`; braking clears buffers/tickets and increments revision. Continuous routes become blocked and require fresh authorization. Operator Stop remains dominant.

The shield uses privileged simulator geometry, velocities and whole-body pose, including arms. These data are not sent to Luna or SmolVLA. It does not classify humans, infer intent, predict abrupt changes, or guarantee avoidance of an obstacle that appears inside the stopping envelope. Rotation and deformable-body prediction are not implemented. A person can still walk into an already stopped robot. Direct manual/single-step drive and manipulation are not covered by this buffered-navigation predictor. This is not a certified real-world pedestrian safety system.

This application is step-locked: idle inference and Stop freeze all physics, including the pedestrian. Continuous Luna supervision now accepts a `wait` decision for 0.5-4 simulated seconds. It holds the current posture using validated worker-owned wait commands of at most 0.5 seconds each, checking cancellation before and after feedback and execution. Wait is not replaced by no-progress exploration. It consumes the existing turn/time budgets and never clears the Stop latch. No background world-stepping thread was introduced.

Luna-selected continuous routes use clearance feedback pauses: a `CLEARANCE_STOP` brakes, clears pending motion and ends that route as blocked before any refill. The supervisor receives fresh feedback and can choose wait, inspection or a newly validated route. Contacts and other runtime failures remain fatal; manual camera-selected routes retain fail-stop behavior. No paused route automatically resumes and no original route budget is renewed. The observed map still retains old dynamic-obstacle evidence, so successful autonomous resumption remains an evaluation question.

Evaluation latches any robot/pedestrian contact per physics tick. Yield requires a grounded base below 0.025 m/s and 0.15 rad/s for 0.4 simulated seconds in the approach area while the pedestrian crosses. Passing is credited only after yielding and the full crossing. Final parking is recomputed from actual grounded, stationary chassis/wheel bounds. Parking after contact cannot recover success without reset. These private checks do not enter model observations.

Focused real-physics regressions cover both fail-stop and feedback-pause braking, the complete crossing without contact, fresh scripted continuation to the bay, Stop rejection, fresh-episode reset, contact-failure latching and sensor isolation. A separate scripted supervisor test approaches an observation-derived floor candidate, receives the moving-obstacle pause, waits four seconds, and earns the two yield/crossing objectives without contact. This does not establish real-model resumption or final parking. `scripts.record_scenario_tests` records a separate known-route safety demonstration through the worker, never the live operator session. It is scripted evidence, not a natural-language autonomy result.

## Live Instructions And Episode Memory

`POST /api/agent/instruction` takes `{run_id, episode_epoch, session_id, message}` for an active `luna_continuous` or `luna_navigation` session. It requires a connected operator, Chat interaction mode, same origin and exclusive transition ownership. Empty text is rejected, messages are capped at 2,000 characters, and obsolete session/episode identifiers return 409.

The controller interrupts and awaits the old execution task before starting the new instruction as the active goal. Physics stays in the same episode. Partial motion is captured from sensor feedback before resuming; input/output token totals, remaining turn budget and the original wall deadline are retained. Each instruction receives a new control session ID, invalidating duplicate submissions from the old session. An interruption revision detects Stop, takeover, reset or disconnect during cancellation/feedback waits; those prevent restart. Exact Stop/Pause/Halt/Cancel messages are handled locally even at an exhausted budget. Non-navigation controllers retain their original chat API.

`NavigationMemory` is owned by the controller and bound to run ID/epoch. It retains at most 64 visited positions, 128 measured view records, 48 action results, eight operator instructions and four model assessments. Model input summaries use the most recent 24 positions, eight actions, four nearby failures and four instructions. View headings are divided into 24 sectors; the summary reports sectors inspected within 0.25 m of the current encoder position and accumulated absolute rotation for movements under 0.08 m near that position. This is coarse odometry-based coverage, not persistent SLAM or a proof that every pixel was inspected.

Both Luna modes receive the same measured history and guidance to distinguish intended from executed movement, avoid unchanged failed routes and preserve legitimate backtracking. Continuous base scans record measured headings and final displacement; SmolVLA steps record the actual instruction, observed result and encoder delta. Landmark notes are explicitly labelled `model_assessment_not_verified`. The original instructions are historical; the newest goal controls the redirect. No spectator geometry or hidden evaluator state is included.

Memory survives control redirection within the episode. The continuous mode keeps the stopped head scan but skips repeated full startup panoramas when executed episode history already exists. Reset/challenge changes clear memory. Successful manual placement explicitly clears spatial memory because its encoder frame does not represent the relocated world position. This update does not introduce a hard prohibition against repeat movements or claim improved real-model success rates.

The UI exposes **Run chat** and **Movement memory** in both navigation modes, using the existing live state stream. Messages show pending/applied/cancelled status and distinguish controller acknowledgements from model reports. The chat composer is enabled only during active connected navigation; it is not a parallel independent robot controller. The transcript is bounded and persists across redirects until episode replacement.

## Luna Continuous Supervision

`guide_continuous.completion_mode` defaults to `parking`, retaining the existing perimeter, surface-match and clearance checks. Object-viewing goals can select `visual_inspection`: `inspect_arrival` or the first `finish` holds the current head/base posture for one simulated second, then requests fresh feedback. A subsequent `finish` with `entry_confirmed=true` accepts the model's visual-inspection claim without a parking rotation. Executed actions and parking inspection invalidate that view check. Target identity and distance are still model judgments; the independent task scorer can reject a false claim. This does not grant movement authorization or expose evaluator targets.

`luna_continuous` is now available beside the unchanged `luna_navigation` SmolVLA mode. Luna receives AgentObservation, paired head images, and candidates/colors/clearance/visit history derived solely from RGB-depth and odometry. No spectator geometry, scene labels, or evaluator state enter its input or destination planner. The conventional tracker, not SmolVLA, supplies wheel velocities through the existing validated worker.

Startup optionally folds arms and performs the existing four-pose floor scan. `AgentStart.adaptive_navigation` defaults true; the Settings checkbox persists per browser. Adaptive startup omits the two full base rotations and offers current reachable candidates first. An adaptive `scan` turns at most 180 degrees toward missing semantic-height coverage, then repeats the local floor scan. View coverage expires after 90 wall seconds or 0.25 m translation; historical panorama images expire after 90 seconds, 0.2 m translation or episode change. Arrival inspection retains its full downward rotation and unchanged surface, entry and parking checks. Disabling adaptive exploration restores the two startup/search sweeps (startup still reuses existing episode history). The earlier full downward sweep expanded a kitchen diagnostic from 37 to 900 connected cells; reduced startup coverage can therefore delay finding some routes. Current RGB and a numbered candidate overlay precede labelled historical views. The trace retains every submitted image. Sampled floor colors roll with the map; colors contain no room labels.

Adaptive supervision counts completed actions without reaching a position at least 0.3 m from its last 64 reached endpoints. Frequent intermediate observations do not reset this counter. After four such actions, the controller can replace a repetitive decision with a current reachable unvisited candidate, a missing-heading scan, or a backtrack whose endpoint is also a currently reachable candidate. Failed targets in the last eight actions are excluded within 0.3 m. Two recovery interventions are permitted before new spatial progress is required; exhaustion holds the robot and reports an unachievable outcome, not proven global impossibility. Recovery is separate from the tracker's existing three-second stall guard. Legitimate revisits can count as no progress, and arrival inspection/finish decisions retain their separate verification and overall time/turn budgets. Controller replacements are identified separately from Luna reports in chat and trace.

Measured successful transitions of at least 0.2 m retain up to 32 start/end pairs. The latest 12 pairs younger than 30 minutes associate endpoint-nearby sighting IDs (within 0.8 m) in `sighting_route_connections`; candidates also expose distance to each sighting. These describe observed viewpoints and executed odometry, not room centers, verified doorways, straight-line paths or current clearance. Sightings remain unverified and cannot authorize motion. Reset/relocation clears this memory; instruction redirection retains episode history and both exploration settings.

Inference is serialized. By default it runs while stopped; **Plan while moving** opts into the bounded continuation protocol below. Stopped internal depth selections may be retained up to 45 seconds only while measured pose displacement stays below 0.05; a fresh map and a newly validated full route are required before motion. Public manual pixel selections keep their one-second limit. Stop revisions, motion tickets, whole-body collision checks and 60-second local deadlines remain. No fixed kitchen route or private success coordinates are used.

The tracker retains velocity through shallow corners, slows for sharp corners, and stops after three seconds without meaningful position or heading progress. Completed routes and rotations perform a validated half-second zero-motion settling command before inference. The whole continuous session uses a 320x240 preview and restores the original dimensions on exit. Stop/disconnect cleanup publishes cancelled buffer state before releasing ownership.

Final completion requires explicit entry confirmation, an arrival inspection since movement, a match to a model-selected observed floor reference, and 8 cm extra observed clearance outside the conservative footprint. These checks remain fallible: Luna can select the wrong room or surface. Only the independent unchanged scorer establishes physical success. Pauses remain for scanning, final parking and missing/invalid continuations; zero buffer stops does not mean uninterrupted whole-task motion. SLAM and dynamic-map clearing remain unimplemented.

### Asynchronous Local Control

Depth acquisition and paired model captures still run on the authoritative physics worker. Map projection, floor-color accumulation and depth-display encoding run in a single background processor on a copied map. Only one job is in flight; the worker never queues a backlog of frames. A completed map is swapped atomically only if its source map is still current and its source capture is at most one second old. Reset, disable and relocation invalidate pending results. Stopped forced scans may wait at most one second for processing; a timeout retains the outstanding job rather than spawning more. Sampling targets five Hz during motion, with actual throughput dependent on capture cost. Idle polling is slower.

Continuous scans prepare the existing bounded snapshot-rendering process before any pixel is selected. The process renders only a copied scene and cannot actuate the real robot. Preview frames older than half a second, from another episode, or behind the last published simulation timestamp are discarded. Model inputs never use this operator preview. Expensive snapshot rendering and PNG encoding therefore leave the continuous-motion critical path; raw depth capture, full-body safety and occasional candidate replanning still cost worker time.

The local tracker targets 50 ms updates. Three scripted real-physics routes measured about 11 updates/second, maximum gaps 140/141/156 ms, arrival error 0.037-0.039 m, and zero buffer starvation. These are unloaded test observations, not a guaranteed 20 Hz or hard-real-time system. Recorded earlier gaps were 266-375 ms but were not collected in a controlled same-code A/B test. Speed limits remain 0.15 m/s and 0.5 rad/s.

If observed lookahead is blocked, the tracker brakes and attempts at most two local replans to the same goal without consulting Luna. Each replacement must stay within the originally anchored observed-support mask and the remaining 1.8 m route budget. The 60-second deadline and runtime travel counters are not renewed. Failed replanning, stale maps, expired leases, contacts and operator Stop still end motion. This is bounded recovery from newly observed geometry, not dynamic-obstacle prediction.

### Plan While Moving

`AgentStart.continuous_handoff` defaults false. The UI checkbox and evaluator `--continuous-handoff` enable it only for continuous supervision; the five-room `flat_kitchen` preset initializes that checkbox on and selects continuous control. During an active route, the worker captures paired sensors and an internal proposal containing route identity/revision, Stop revision and measured pose; candidate planning runs off-worker. Luna can propose a compatible observed continuation while the robot keeps following its already-authorized route. Only one model request is outstanding.

Moving proposals reuse the exact 160x120 RGB from their depth acquisition, with a same-pose/time AgentObservation and frame-cache reference; they do not render a second RGB image. Stopped feedback and the independent operator preview retain their existing resolutions. Spatial map processing copies its mutable arrays independently without recursively copying the old observation, which the new frame replaces. Floor rasterization batches coordinate conversion and discards only polygons wholly outside the map; pixel-equivalence tests preserve observed/unknown boundaries. This reduces avoidable processing work without changing the one-second depth-age guard or allowing stale motion.

Acceptance rechecks episode, route identity/revision, Stop, live buffer, fresh map, deadline, at most 15-second proposal age and at most 0.9 m encoder translation. The worker replans from the current pose, checks the original support mask and current full-body clearance, and rejects a first-leg heading change greater than 0.35 rad. At most two handoffs are allowed, and travelled distance plus the new route must remain within the ORIGINAL 1.8 m budget. Neither velocity nor deadline is reset. This deliberately does not permit endless goal extensions.

Stop, arrival and local replanning invalidate old proposals. Late/invalid proposals do not restart a stopped route. Inspection and finish replies obtained while moving are deferred until fresh stopped feedback. When no valid continuation arrives, normal arrival braking remains. Scripted delayed-inference tests prove overlap and late-reply cancellation; no new real-Luna success-rate improvement is claimed. SmolVLA weights, its primitive adapter and its resident lifetime are unchanged. Short-chunk learned control remains a separate experiment.

Sensing preference survives reset/challenge changes within a live backend, but maps and frame URLs do not. New server lifetimes start disabled. Enable is allowed during model control and is idempotent; disable and actual manual scans remain ownership-protected. The browser remembers the fold preference; merely changing it never actuates the arms.

## Spatial Sensing

Spatial sensing is a separate typed `SpatialObservation`. The legacy Luna/SmolVLA policy retains its original RGB/proprioception contract; the new continuous supervisor consumes observation-derived candidates as described above. Physics authority and evaluator isolation remain unchanged.

- **Capture:** one PyBullet TinyRenderer call produces synchronized RGB, depth and an internal segmentation buffer at 160x120, 65-degree vertical FOV, near 0.015 m and far 12 m. The normalized depth buffer is converted to optical-axis meters; values beyond 4 m, invalid/no-return values, and robot pixels become null. Segmentation is used only to identify the robot itself, with a one-pixel dilation margin; scene IDs and link IDs are discarded and never exposed. RGB is unmodified. The matching depth PNG is a display-only near-bright grayscale image with transparent invalid pixels; the JSON depth values retain metric precision.
- **Pairing:** each pair carries one sequence, episode, monotonic capture timestamp, simulated time, intrinsics, measured head angles and wheel odometry. Capture is serialized on the physics worker and neither steps physics nor increments model observation sequences. The normal 640x480 preview is independent; the spatial panel uses its own exact 160x120 RGB/depth pair, never a later normal-preview frame.
- **Projection:** optical x-right/y-down/z-forward points are transformed using calibrated robot/head geometry and encoder-relative pose. No world position, hidden obstacle geometry, object identity or evaluator coordinate enters the mapper. Robot forward kinematics assume a flat floor and nominal chassis height 0.155 m; base tilt, wheel slip and accumulated odometry error are not estimated. This is not visual odometry or SLAM.
- **Map:** an 8x8 m rolling grid uses 5 cm cells in the wheel-odometry frame. Observed near-floor endpoints (-4 to +4 cm height) and small quads supported by adjacent floor-depth pixels mark floor; obstacle endpoints above 4 cm and below 1.2 m mark obstacles. Obstacle evidence dominates floor evidence. Invalid/self-masked pixels and floor behind occluders are not ray-cleared. Unseen space stays unknown. Old obstacle evidence remains until it leaves the rolling window or the map resets; dynamic-object clearing and loop closure are future work. The raw grid still reports `planning_ready=false`; the opt-in continuous controller separately derives a conservative footprint-clearance mask before authorizing a local path.
- **Freshness:** map updates reject mismatched episodes, duplicate/out-of-order sequences, regressing timestamps, future captures and frames older than one second. Asynchronous sampling targets 5 Hz during motion; idle polling targets about 4 Hz, with no physics advancement. Manipulation rendering pauses it. Reset preserves the enabled preference but invalidates the old map/URLs. Disable clears map/cache. Manual relocation clears the old map because encoder odometry intentionally does not reflect operator placement.
- **Footprint:** the panel publishes conservative base-frame bounds of current robot links, including arms and grippers, not a chassis-only rectangle. Only the robot's own geometry is used. Continuous planning inflates obstacles and unknown space by a circle enclosing these bounds plus grid margin.

API: `POST /api/spatial` takes `{run_id, episode_epoch, enabled}`; enable is allowed during model ownership, while disable requires idle manual ownership. `GET /api/spatial` returns metadata/map/footprint and freshness. Eight recent pairs are retained at `/api/spatial/{run_id}/{sequence}/rgb.png`, `depth.png`, and `depth.json`; disabled, expired or previous-episode references return 404. Responses are no-store. The browser fetches matched pairs and displays both only after decoding. Native Windows and existing NumPy/SciPy/PyBullet dependencies suffice.

**Travel posture:** calibration includes the optional six-joint target `[0, -1.3, 2.6, 0, 0, 0]` for each arm (`camera-clear-fold-v1`). The shoulder/elbow fold is unchanged; wrist pitch is now 0 rather than -1.3 rad, tucking the grippers downward instead of pointing their tips into the lower camera corners. Tests move each arm through the existing collision-checked `set_arm_joints` command over two seconds, verify a reduced forward envelope and no contact in parking, kitchen and recharge scenes, and check safe unfolding plus hand/chassis/opposite-arm separation. A straight-ahead camera at head pitch 0, 0.2 and 0.45 rad has zero visible arm-link pixels in the 640x480 reference render; the enhanced default 0.45-rad image is also checked visually after the old-to-new fold. The camera pose, 65-degree field of view, resolution, self-mask and physical/visual geometry are unchanged. Sideways views and steep downward inspection scans can still see the robot; this is not an all-head-angle guarantee. The default URDF/neutral posture is unchanged. Do not force this target from an arbitrary pose or with a carried object; ordinary trajectory validation remains required. This limited test does not certify all self-collision combinations or obstacle configurations. Existing backends need a restart to load the new target; folding still occurs only through an authorized scan/command. [Camera and fixed-baseline evidence](DESIGN_PERFORMANCE.md).

## Continuous Local Navigation

This opt-in operator-selected mode uses the existing worker and NavigationRuntime, not the fixed four-skill `navigation_plan` LLM protocol. It makes no model calls. `POST /api/continuous/scan` takes the current run/epoch and optional `compact_arms`; it performs four bounded head scans while stopped, optionally moving each empty arm through the already validated travel target first. Every joint movement uses existing command validation, and Stop interrupts the scan. `POST /api/continuous/start` takes run/epoch, retained spatial sequence and normalized RGB pixel. A connected operator and exclusive idle/manual ownership are required.

The chosen pixel must have valid depth on the observed floor, be captured within one second, and remain within 0.05 measured pose displacement. The target is projected using that frame's calibrated camera and odometry, never a hidden target or spectator coordinate. Routes are 0.15-1.8 m local destinations; path length is at most 1.8 m. No automatic goal refresh or travel-limit reset occurs while following one destination.

For connectivity, the mapper now uses all valid depth pixels and rasterizes only small floor quads supported by four adjacent valid near-floor pixels, with edges no longer than 0.2 m. Invalid/self-occluded pixels and depth discontinuities do not produce quads. Obstacle evidence still dominates. The planner uses a conservative circular radius enclosing the current whole robot, not just its chassis, with grid discretization margin. It blocks unknown and obstacle cells after clearance erosion. There is one explicit start bootstrap: the currently occupied start disk plus two grid cells is accepted as the starting support region, anchored for this goal only; obstacle cells still override it. This bridges unavoidable near-body self-occlusion, does not fill the published map, and does not grant a free-space corridor to the destination. Independent whole-robot collision and floor guards still run during all motion.

SciPy sparse Dijkstra finds a connected route over this grid; visibility-checked shortcuts remove unnecessary grid corners. The tracker follows measured wheel odometry, refreshing two one-second velocity segments at a 0.05-second target interval while preserving velocity. Acceleration limits remain 0.3 m/s squared and 1 rad/s squared; continuous command speed is capped at 0.5 m/s and 0.5 rad/s. Legacy navigation and SmolVLA remain limited to 0.15 m/s. Forward range hits additionally limit requested speed to fit braking distance plus 50 ms of travel and a 0.10 m margin, leaving 0.02 m beyond the independent range-stop margin. This prevents a higher cruise request from repeatedly triggering the safety brake near posts; it does not bypass that brake or treat missing range returns as proven free space. Both current and proposed motion are checked against observed traversability over a one-second lookahead. The runtime independently enforces full-body clearance, supporting floor, battery, lease, per-tick Stop and the 60-second deadline.

Arrival within 0.04 m brakes deterministically. Blocked lookahead permits only the bounded same-goal replans above. Stale depth, failed replanning, buffer starvation, expired authorization or runtime faults end the goal. The controller never repeats stale velocities to hide missed deadlines. Sharp direction changes still require slowdown or rotation.

The worker is the sole motor writer; it updates the tracker between physics slices without an LLM wait. During this mode the asynchronous head-camera preview uses 320x240 and restores its prior dimensions on completion/cancellation. Spatial sensing stays 160x120. Measured clear-route cadence is described above; actual timing depends on capture, safety checking and scheduling.

`continuous_navigation` and spatial `continuous` report route, remaining distance, updates, buffer stops, local replans, moving handoffs and maximum update gap. They are not task-completion claims. The supervisor also receives this measured execution feedback. Competing starts are blocked while busy; Stop/takeover/reset/final operator disconnect end motion. Legacy Luna/SmolVLA remains separate. Robust room search, SLAM and dynamic-map clearing remain future work.

## Ownership and Timing

The opt-in `supervised_policy` mode is an exception to the original rendering path below: a worker-owned left-arm task executor accepts local SmolVLA action chunks, while a separate snapshot process renders 320x240 paired head-camera feedback. Supervisor and policy inference are independent. See [SMOLVLA.md](SMOLVLA.md) for contracts, limits, freshness, and the trained-checkpoint gate. No additional process can actuate the authoritative Bullet client. Single-step and navigation retain the original timing behavior described here.

`SimulationWorker` owns one serialized thread. It initializes, executes, renders, and disposes `BulletSimulation`. FastAPI awaits concurrent futures; it does not execute physics on the event loop. A thread-safe event interrupts motion at a physics tick without waiting for the command queue. Reset closes the old worker before constructing the next episode.

Policy motion uses `PolicyPacer` with absolute high-resolution deadlines. A requested Windows 4.2 ms event wait was measured taking about 19 ms, causing former per-tick relative sleeps to accumulate and expire valid chunks. The pacer accounts for oversleep using subsequent ticks, rebases after more than 50 ms of lag or a motion-revision change, and resets on an empty buffer. Waits remain interruptible; each physics tick still checks cancellation and unmodified chunk/skill wall-clock deadlines. Fixed 1/240 s integration, collision checks, snapshots every 12 ticks, and the single motion authority are unchanged. This improves aggregate pacing but does not guarantee evenly spaced or hard-real-time ticks. See [SMOLVLA.md](SMOLVLA.md#windows-policy-timing-fix) for measured before/after reports.

Each episode owns an authoritative dynamic PyBullet DIRECT client and a private, unstepped, fixed-base planning client. The planner receives copies of state for kinematic/collision validation. It is not a second authoritative simulation, and no frontend physics is used. Spectator geometry comes from the same generated URDF visual shapes as the physics robot, with server poses applied to links.

Physics advances at 1/240 s only during commands or a valid navigation buffer (`step_locked` outside those intervals). Live manual and model-directed execution is wall-paced by the worker. State snapshots publish every 12 ticks (20 Hz simulated); the operator head-camera preview targets 10 Hz wall-clock capture during commands, with initial and final frames also published. Actual throughput depends on CPU rendering and browser delivery, not a hard real-time guarantee. Navigation mode permits bounded motion during inference, not free-running physics after a buffer expires. The final browser disconnect sets the stop event; reconnect does not automatically resume motion.

Preview capture runs on the physics worker through `capture()`, without calling `observe()` or changing model observation sequences. A separate 16-frame cache serves run-scoped `/api/camera/{run_id}/{reference}` PNGs with `no-store`; reset invalidates old URLs. The browser keeps the last decoded image while loading one replacement at a time and skips intermediate previews when delivery is slow. Model feedback still captures its own paired observation/image at scheduled boundaries, and the exchange feed retains those exact inputs independently. Spectator poses and preview metadata remain operator-only.

Chat and Voice selection is server-side operator state. `POST /api/agent/mode` validates the run ID/epoch, stops the worker, and awaits controller cancellation before changing modes. Text starts and `POST /api/agent/chat` require Chat; new voice sockets require Voice. Existing same-origin, exclusive ownership, operator-connection, and stale-episode checks apply. Leaving Voice unmounts the audio client immediately while server cancellation finishes. Mode selection never starts inference or recording by itself.

Chat reuses the Responses controller and canonical tools. A strict `ChatStart` adds a nonblank message (maximum 2,000 characters) and optional prior conversation ID. Follow-ups validate that ID and episode before retaining complete model/tool turns within the selected estimated token budget. Each message is serialized, captures fresh robot feedback, and gets the existing response/time limits; text-only replies finish without motion. The active typed request remains in the current goal even with zero retained history. Visible user/assistant messages are bounded to 40 entries. Other than the user's text and public goal, world inputs remain restricted to `AgentObservation` and its paired head image. Chat and Voice histories are not merged.

`backend/feedback.py` serializes model-facing sensor values, tool results, and recent-action summaries with at most three decimal places and no JSON whitespace. Whole numbers and negative zero are normalized; integer identifiers, strings, and booleans are unchanged. Physics, validation, safety thresholds, raw API observations, and executed commands retain their original precision. Rounded zero is not proof of zero movement. Compact tool replies retain status/errors, duration, measured deltas, observation sequence, and collision feedback instead of repeating the full observation and action ID; fresh paired camera/sensor feedback follows separately. Recent actions omit redundant timestamps, descriptions, and durations. Image detail remains high. The exchange feed shows the rounded sensor payload, detailed result, and exact compact reply sent to the model; numeric compaction applies to Foundry, Ollama, and Realtime voice.

Text control uses `AgentStart.images_per_request` (1-8, default 1) and `context_tokens` (0-32768, default 4096). `camera_batch` includes the current paired observation/image first, then distinct historical pairs selected from the controller's last eight feedback observations, ordered by capture timestamp. Repeated PNGs are omitted, so the batch can be smaller than requested. No spectator or live-preview cache metadata is used. Historical pairs carry the explicit `historical_camera_observation` label; only the current observation can authorize motion. The charging preset reserves one historical slot for its initial view when the image limit is at least two and that view differs from the current image. New runs/reset clear camera memory; valid typed follow-ups and idle wake retain it within the same episode.

`retain_context` removes image parts from saved turns and retains the newest whole turns that fit the remaining budget after recent-action summaries. A conservative cross-provider text-token estimate counts UTF-8 JSON bytes plus 16 units per message; this is deliberately not billed token usage or a model-specific tokenizer count. The budget excludes current inputs, tool schemas, instructions, and the selected camera pairs. Zero omits historical text and summaries. Trimmed turns are removed from memory and cannot reappear after increasing the budget; a 200-turn guard bounds object count. The trace records the effective budget, estimate, actual image count, and each image's frame/time metadata. Indexed trace image URLs share session/retention/reset checks with the original single-frame URL. Voice remains on its existing Realtime conversation handling.

## Reactive Navigation Execution

`AgentStart.execution_mode` defaults to `single_step`. `navigation_plan` selects five tools: `set_navigation_plan`, `replace_motion_buffer`, `complete_navigation_skill`, `observe`, and `stop`. The first three have strict contracts in `backend/navigation.py`; `/api/tools?execution_mode=navigation_plan` exposes the same schemas. Foundry and Ollama share the executor and rules. Standard tools and manual operation retain their previous contracts. No manipulation tool can run while navigation owns motion.

Plans contain one to eight steps following the repeated order inspect_room, locate_doorway, approach, cross. The runtime tracks pending/running/completed/failed/cancelled states. Replacing a plan brakes and cancels its remaining skills before beginning the new plan. Inspection permits head segments only; locating also permits pure base rotation. Completing either requires at least 0.4 rad of measured head-plus-base scan. Approach and crossing require at least 0.1 m net encoder translation, no current contact, and an evidence string from the model. These are mechanical preconditions, not a visual doorway classifier. Travel is capped at 2 m per approach and 1.5 m for other skills. Each active skill has a 60-second wall-clock deadline.

The worker advances navigation in slices of up to 12 physics ticks and accepts queued operations between slices. A buffer contains one to four segments of 0.2-1.0 s each, up to 2.0 s total, controlling either the head or the base. Base limits are 0.15 m/s and 0.5 rad/s, with target-velocity slew limits of 0.3 m/s squared and 1 rad/s squared. The remaining horizon also ramps velocity down before buffer exhaustion. Replacement keeps the current velocity and changes only future setpoints. There is no new free-running physics thread or second motion authority.

`AgentObservation.navigation` reports only the submitted plan and its measured execution telemetry, not scene/evaluator geometry. Feedback creates a worker-owned ticket binding observation sequence, plan revision, capture time, and encoder pose. Updates must provide the current revision. A moving observation expires after 2 s; a stationary one after 45 s. Either is rejected after more than 0.25 m encoder translation or 0.35 rad heading change. Exhaustion, replacement, skill transition, Stop, and faults invalidate earlier tickets. Old observation IDs are not silently reassigned to new state.

The controller compares both remaining simulated motion and remaining wall-clock lease against a low-water threshold of `min(1.5, max(0.75, last_inference_latency + 0.25))` seconds, while respecting the selected minimum feedback interval. If the estimated inference latency plus 0.25 s cannot fit the available window, it waits for the buffer to brake before capturing feedback and planning from rest. Faster inference can still run concurrently with motion. If expiry or exhaustion changes the revision during inference, the outdated request is cancelled and awaited before capturing fresh feedback; its tool calls are never submitted. These retries count toward the existing turn/session limits, but not toward the three-consecutive-invalid-tool-update limit. Navigation retains complete turns within the same selected text budget as single-step mode; the latest observation contains the current plan state. Inference remains serialized.

Before each slice, the worker checks fresh proximity/contact data, supporting floor contact, and base tilt within 0.25 rad. Whole-robot collision preflight samples a 0.4-second lookahead at four points for both requested and current velocity, using a 15 mm scene clearance and the floor footprint. Translational distance beams below 0.18 m also stop motion. The private planning client is used internally for safety; object identities and coordinates are not returned to the model. This conservative constant-velocity sampling is not a complete dynamic planner or certified stopping-distance guarantee.

The buffer wall-clock lease is its submitted duration plus 0.5 s (at most 2.5 s), unchanged by slower rendering or model latency. Both lease expiry and normal exhaustion brake, discard pending motion, and invalidate tickets, leaving the active skill awaiting fresh feedback; neither is task completion or a fatal plan error. A local feedback gap over 0.75 s, contact/clearance/stability failure, execution exception, or skill timeout still brakes and fails the active skill. Later steps are cancelled. The original 60-second skill deadline is not renewed by buffer pauses. Stop remains independently checked each physics tick, and its cleared buffer is published immediately. The controller notices worker failure while waiting for a model and cancels that request without waiting for its ordinary timeout. Ended navigation runs do not arm automatic camera wake. These software mechanisms depend on the worker making progress and do not establish hardware emergency-stop safety.

## Idle and Camera Wake

Completed text/chat tasks arm a separate, cancellable local watcher after the inference client closes. Successful scenario completion/failure stops goal-driven continuation; a `stop` tool may supply `task_status` (`completed` or `unachievable`) and a reason. Text-only replies also arm the watcher. Voice arms it after a final reply, preserving the existing open connection. Errors and hard operator interrupts do not arm automatic retries.

The watcher samples `capture()` on the serialized worker every 0.5 s without calling `observe()` or stepping Bullet. RGB images are resized to 64 x 48; a change means at least 1% of pixels differ from the last meaningful reference by more than 12 levels in any channel. The reference is retained across sub-threshold noise so gradual changes can accumulate. These thresholds are a local motion heuristic, not semantic understanding. Only a changed sample publishes a new operator preview.

After at least five wall-clock seconds since arming and since the last meaningful image change, a completed task enters `sleeping`. Joints remain held and wheels braked; the programmatic stop latch is released only if the worker's stop revision is unchanged. No inference runs in idle. A new camera revision wakes once, then disarms that watcher while fresh feedback is processed. Text wakes reuse session ID, token totals, bounded history, seen call IDs, feedback pacing, remaining turn budget, and original deadline. They do not create unlimited fresh sessions. Voice camera wake is enforced with tools disabled and does not record audio.

New Chat/Voice interaction cancels the watcher before user-requested control. Stop revisions and cancellation checks protect queued resume and wake operations. Stop, takeover, mode changes, reset, disconnect, and text-model reconfiguration invalidate watchers; no camera event may resume their canceled motion. Camera-monitor failure stops control. Watcher metadata is operator-only and is never inserted into `AgentObservation`; model inputs remain real paired observations/images plus user text. Physics stays step-locked, and there is no camera-change trigger from spectator-only navigation.

## Coordinate Frames

- World and base: x forward, y left, z up at zero heading.
- Base origin: chassis center, approximately 0.155 m above a level floor.
- Quaternions: x,y,z,w. Accept norm within 0.02 of one, normalize it, and reject all other/degenerate inputs.
- Head camera: rigid offset `[0.095, 0, 0.005]` m from head link frame; forward is head +x and up is head +z. Positive head pitch tilts down.
- Camera optical: right = head -y, down = head -z, forward = head +x.
- Three.js spectator: a -pi/2 rotation around x maps `(x,y,z)` to `(x,z,-y)` in the y-up renderer. Bullet cylinder z axes are explicitly converted to Three.js cylinder y axes. Desktop/mobile renderer and motion checks use real canvas pixels.

The camera is rendered with Bullet TinyRenderer at 640 x 480, vertical FOV 65 degrees, near 0.015 m, far 12 m. There is no depth/segmentation payload in agent observations. Camera self-occlusion uses the robot's actual geometry. Spectator orbit controls cannot change the camera transform on the backend.

Both arms attach to the torso through fixed shoulder brackets with matching visual and collision geometry. Each bracket bridges the torso to the shoulder pivot at `[0, +/-0.25, 0.245]` m in the base frame. These pivots, joint axes, link lengths, and calibrated palm targets are unchanged. Collision preflight includes both brackets; only the first two joints' intentional overlap with their own mount is excluded.

## Surface Materials

`backend/materials.py` deterministically generates six original 512 x 512 RGB PNGs into `assets/textures` when missing: concrete, plaster, wood, tile, fabric, and stone. Scene names select materials; task cubes and goal markings retain their identifying solid colors. PyBullet applies the maps to authoritative visual shapes, and geometry metadata supplies the same allowlisted `/api/textures/{name}.png` URLs to Three.js. The renderer disposes textures on scene replacement and adds soft shadow mapping. UV mapping and lighting differ between renderers, but geometry, surface patterns, and physical state are shared. Materials do not change friction, collision geometry, or task scoring.

## Proximity Sensors

`AgentObservation.proximity` contains eight `DistanceReading` values and a list of directional `CollisionReading` forces. Rays are cast in the authoritative Bullet client at 45-degree intervals around the base frame, starting at the rectangular sensor envelope (x +/-0.18 m, y +/-0.25 m) and z=-0.115 m relative to the base. Readings are distance from each mount to first hit, rounded to millimeters, with a 2 m maximum. They rotate with the robot. `clear` and `occluded` return a null distance, distinguishing no hit from self-occlusion. A beam at one low height cannot represent the full arm/body swept volume or guarantee obstacle clearance.

Contact sensors group physical normal forces above 0.05 N into front/left/rear/right using the robot-local contact position. They exclude self-contact, floor contact, and finger-to-movable-object grasp contact, but include finger contact with static obstacles. The existing `bumpers` list now contains these contact directions instead of flagging every chassis contact. Only directions, bearings, distances, status, simulated timestamp, and forces are serialized; hit bodies and positions remain private.

The worker publishes a separate operator `proximity` snapshot every 12 physics ticks and after actions/placement. This does not advance observation sequence or increase model feedback cadence. Single-step actions also retain the peak directional contact forces sampled every 12 ticks (20 Hz simulated) in `sensor_deltas.contacts_during_action`, so contact that clears before the final observation can still reach the model. This is a sampled peak, not every instantaneous impact. The accumulator resets for each action, including cancellation. It exposes no object identities or world coordinates.

Model inputs include concise collision guidance derived from those measurements. Current contacts take precedence over historical contacts; front/side contact suggests inspecting rear clearance and considering a short, slow reverse, while current rear contact explicitly forbids reversing. Distance beams alone do not establish clearance. Cleared action contacts are labeled `during_action` rather than reported as current contact. A rejected preflight is not fabricated as a physical collision. Advice does not execute motion, release Stop, or override navigation safety failures. Actual recovery remains a model decision through the validated executor.

After an otherwise successful `drive_base`, the simulator reports `NO_PROGRESS` when the final bumper/contact list is nonempty, the request magnitude times elapsed simulation time is at least 0.02 m or 0.08 rad, and encoder translation is below 0.005 m and heading change below 0.02 rad. The action has already run and braked; its actual duration and observed encoder deltas are retained. Zero-motion waits, observation/head movement, and successful retreat remain available. The heuristic uses no evaluator pose, cannot reliably detect wheel slip, and is not predictive obstacle avoidance.

The text/Ollama controller summarizes its last three completed actions into the latest feedback message, alongside the corresponding camera and observation, and records the same summary in the trace. The summary is not stored again in rolling model history, preventing recursive duplication. Three consecutive `drive_base` results with `NO_PROGRESS` stop that controller with an error and no automatic wake. This does not establish semantic recovery or add equivalent retry counting to Realtime voice, which still receives the underlying tool error. The worker publishes final observation/result and `busy=false` together to avoid reporting a completed observation as in-flight.

## Outcome Feedback

Text and Voice controllers retain an operator-only outcome record with kind, reason, source, and timestamp. Sources distinguish `physics`, `agent`, and `controller`. Reported completion/unachievable states do not alter private evaluator scores. Controller errors, limits, interruptions, and text-only endings have separate classifications. Outcomes persist through idle, reset on a new request or automatic reassessment, and retain explicit operator interruption reasons.

The result band uses current scenario checks for green verified completion, marks manual-placement intervention, and exposes reported outcomes without presenting them as verified success. Newer tool results can supersede older controller reports. Disconnection and errors override success, reset clears outcomes, and losing scenario conditions removes the success banner. The feedback is local current-state reporting, not a persistent run journal.

## Controls and Limits

The canonical registry is in `backend/contracts.py`; `/api/tools` publishes its JSON Schemas, descriptions, limits, and units. `/api/calibration` publishes joint names/axes, limits, neutral poses, wheel geometry, and an asset hash. `finish_task` and `submit_answer` contracts are defined but are not enabled by these presets. Challenge progress is computed privately from physics and displayed to the operator; a model's summary does not establish success.

- Base commands: at most 0.3 m/s, 0.8 rad/s, and 2 seconds, with a 0.3-second wheel-speed ramp at both ends and 5 Nm wheel motor force limits.
- Arms: six joints each, 1.8 rad/s velocity and 24 Nm force limits. Joint targets exceeding rate/position limits are rejected.
- Head: yaw [-1.5,1.5], pitch [-0.7,1.15] rad, 5 Nm motor force and 1.8 rad/s servo limit.
- Gripper opening: 0 to 0.11 m, with fingers limited to 0.15 m/s and at most 35 N each.
- IK endpoint tolerance: 8 mm / 0.08 rad. Cartesian execution tolerance: 15 mm / 0.1 rad under motor load.
- Cartesian commands snapshot the base frame on acceptance, solve a sequence spaced at most 15 mm apart, validate joint velocities and collision samples before moving, then servo the sequence. They do not search for an alternative route around an obstacle.

The visible palm origin is the grasp center. To reach the curated left floor cube from the initial base pose, use `[0.36,0.25,-0.12]` m with quaternion `[0,0,0,1]`, duration 2 s. Close the left gripper at 20 N, then move the palm to `[0.36,0.25,0.05]` m over 2 s. Open to 0.11 m to release. Right-arm physics tests use a mirrored cube at y=-0.25 m; the default manual bench currently spawns only the left cube.

## Manual Placement

The spectator raycasts visible robot meshes using the operator-only `robot_body_id`. A selected robot is previewed with a translated Three.js group on the world floor plane; the physics scene and camera do not change during the gesture. Background gestures retain OrbitControls. Pointer capture supports mouse and touch; cancellation, Escape, stop, disconnect, a changed observation, or a replaced episode clears the preview. Arrow keys provide 0.1 m world-X/Y steps, with Enter to commit.

`POST /api/robot/placement` accepts a strict `ManualPlacement` containing run ID, epoch, observation sequence, and finite world X/Y coordinates. This route is separate from the model tool registry, requires idle manual ownership, and executes through the serialized worker. It rejects stopped/stale requests and active assisted grasps. The synchronized planning client checks all robot links against the floor footprint and destination obstacle penetration before authoritative mutation. This is teleport-style setup, not a collision-checked travel trajectory.

An accepted drop preserves base height/orientation and joint positions, holds joints, brakes wheels, and zeroes velocities. Scene objects stay in place. Physics time, encoder odometry, battery travel, and challenge history are not reset or advanced by placement. A new observation invalidates old commands, prior cached command results are cleared, and camera/pose snapshots refresh. `manual_placements` records operator intervention until reset; challenge completion in a repositioned episode is not evidence of autonomous performance. Neither placement coordinates nor intervention metadata enter `AgentObservation`.

## Predefined Challenge Checks

`backend/challenges.py` defines ten fixed presets, plus the unscored practice bench. Catalog category/difficulty are operator metadata. Their colored zones and room floor tints are visual-only markings shared by TinyRenderer and the spectator; they do not add a collision plane or support objects. Loading a preset reconstructs both authoritative and planning clients. Reset restarts the selected preset. `floor_size_m` defaults to 6 x 6 m, with each side constrained to 4-20 m. Warehouse uses 10 x 8 m; Service Gallery uses 8 x 8 m. Floor collision bounds, planning copies and spectator geometry share those dimensions; no floor/map coordinates are added to model observations.

Progress is evaluated on the worker at 20 Hz during motion and after each command. Placement requires a previous lift with the object's collision AABB bottom at least 0.08 m above the floor, while grasp-assisted or under real opposing finger contact. Completion requires release, the full horizontal AABB within the target, actual floor contact, linear speed below 0.025 m/s, and angular speed below 0.15 rad/s. Parking uses the combined chassis/wheel AABB with the same rest/contact tests. Basic parking/manipulation do not impose timed dwell; search and selected ordered-route objectives do. Their exact measurements and object identities are not part of `AgentObservation`.

Clinic and Warehouse use ordered objectives: only the current checkpoint can advance and at most one stage advances per update. Every checkpoint needs floor contact and full chassis/wheel containment; a transit checkpoint permits motion, while parking requires rest. Intermediate stages remain recorded. The final objective is re-evaluated each update and becomes incomplete on departure, loss of contact or motion. Optional `dwell_s` (0-10 s, default zero) accrues only on eligible simulation updates and resets when conditions fail; repeated observations at the same simulation time do not add dwell. Warehouse dispatch requires one second. Operator placement can still satisfy physical regions and is explicitly marked as intervention; these checks do not prove autonomous routing.

Warehouse has two offset rack rows with shelves, uprights, stock boxes, lane markings, packing equipment and dispatch terminal. Its blue/orange/green ordered route is verified by real wheel commands over 20 m. Service Gallery has electrical cabinets, vents, tanks, valves, partitions and red/yellow inspection stations; it reuses the search camera/frustum/ray and stationary-dwell evaluator below. The verified wheel route visits the decoy before the target and exceeds 15 m. Workshop adds fixed fixture blocks, a divider, parts shelves, bins, a counter and vise around the existing two-arm cube geometry. Both physical pickup/release routes pass with static fixture poses unchanged, using the standard assisted-grasp approximation. This is not a physical-only learned-policy result.

The clinic description now calls the colored panels landmarks and explicitly excludes carrying supplies: no supply payload or sign-reading classifier exists. Its two private route gates and final bay are verified; final parking is no longer permanently latched. None of the new grounds adds a semantic room classifier or target coordinates to text/voice input.

The return-to-charge preset adds a staged simulated energy budget. Charge starts at 100%, drains by 4 percentage points per meter of wheel-encoder travel plus 0.1 per simulated second off the stationary charger, and recharges at 20 points per simulated second while fully parked on the original pad. One second of stationary survey-zone occupancy applies a one-time 60-point scan cost. Low battery is 35% or below. Survey completion and low-battery return are recorded; final success requires at least 90% while still docked. Charge is clamped to [0,100], and zero charge rejects movement and interrupts an in-flight movement at the next battery update. Stop remains independently interruptible at every physics tick.

Battery state advances only with physics, including the bounded `wait` action (up to two seconds); inference latency, feedback delays, and repeated observations do not recharge or drain it. `wait` brakes and holds all joints, obeys Stop, and uses the same stale-command/deduplication rules as movement. The battery sensor exposes only `charge_pct`, `low`, and `charging`. This is a scaled challenge mechanic, not an electrical, thermal, or battery-chemistry simulation.

Apartment Search is a fixed three-room flat with two 1.3 m doorways, collision walls, simple furniture, an 18 cm yellow cube on a 34 cm pedestal, and a red distractor. Its private `search_target` selects a body; no target zone is rendered. An overhead spectator view exposes the layout only to the operator. Door openings have no movable leaves or headers, keeping the roofless layout readable.

The search evaluator requires planar base-to-target distance <= 0.9 m, floor contact, base speed < 0.025 m/s, angular speed < 0.15 rad/s, and both head-joint speeds < 0.05 rad/s. It samples five points around the target center: at least three must lie inside the actual head-camera frustum with a 5% edge margin, and their first physics ray hit must be the target. Conditions must hold for one continuous simulated second; a failed check resets dwell. Repeated observations or wall-clock inference delays cannot satisfy it. These private measurements never enter `AgentObservation`. This is a curated geometric visibility test, not semantic image recognition or a generalized object-finding benchmark.

Kitchen to Bathroom reuses the apartment walls and open doorways with different room furnishings. Its private `initial_xy` starts both physics clients at [1.1, -1.5] m facing the kitchen appliances, with head pitch 0.12 rad. Clinic, Warehouse and Service Gallery also specify private starting positions; other presets retain [0, 0]. Settling still resets simulation time and encoder odometry to zero. Fixtures are static boxes and cylinders with matching visual/collision shapes; explicit allowlisted material overrides assign tile and fabric using the existing bitmap textures. The toilet bowl/seat, burners, and faucets use cylinders. The mirror-like panel is a flat colored surface, not a reflection renderer.

The bathroom objective has `visible_zone=false`, suppressing its marker while retaining private AABB scoring. Its clear arrival region is centered at [1.40, 1.15] m with size [1.20, 1.48] m. The full chassis/wheel AABB must fit, with actual floor contact, linear speed < 0.025 m/s, and angular speed < 0.15 rad/s. No dwell or head orientation is required, and leaving clears completion. Initial pose, fixture names, room identity, arrival coordinates, and evaluator progress are absent from model observations and text/voice instructions. The public goal asks for visual room identification; this preset scores physical arrival, not the correctness of a natural-language room description.

## Assisted Grasp Approximation

Closing does not select a nearest object. A candidate must be movable, no more than 0.35 kg, and at most 0.11 m in each dimension. Both finger links must exert contact on that same body, their contact normals must oppose (dot product <= -0.7), and its center must lie within the configured palm enclosure.

Only then is a fixed constraint created at the existing relative transform. No object pose is reset or teleported. Object collisions remain active. The constraint is force-limited to 18 N; measured overload or separation beyond 0.12 m drops the object. Opening above 0.065 m releases the constraint. This opening threshold is currently calibrated for the 0.06 m demo cube, not a general release policy for all possible object sizes.

Agent-facing gripper sensors contain only aperture, two contact booleans, and load, never body IDs, names, colors, or poses. `contact_physics` is not offered.

## Remaining Mechanical Risks

- Current collision preflight checks moving links against scene bodies, chassis, torso, head, shoulder brackets, and the opposite arm. Same-arm nonadjacent self-collision coverage and carried-object swept-volume checks need expansion.
- Support spheres approximate low-friction casters; they are not articulated caster assemblies.
- Only the curated cube dimensions, mass, floor positions, and tested motions are verified. General ground workspace, random scene validation, stability margins, and load/drop stress tests remain open.
- No persistent action journal exists yet; action deduplication is per live episode and exact duplicate IDs return the prior result.
- The operator state endpoint is privileged; only `AgentObservation` is intended for future model adapters.
- The API is a local prototype with same-origin checks, not a production authentication boundary.
